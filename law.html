The Law · Policy & Governance

The first jurisdiction built for agentic commerce.

On July 1, 2026, the West Virginia DUNA Act takes effect. WV becomes the first — and only — place on Earth where you can register a Decentralized Unincorporated Nonprofit Association with the Secretary of State.

01 · The legislation

The DUNA Act.

West Virginia just did something no state has done. It wrote a law that gives crypto projects, AI agents, and the communities they belong to a real legal home — not a workaround, not a gray area, but a recognized entity in statute.

The statutory gap it closes — before this law, a decentralized organization had two choices: pretend it didn't exist, or stuff itself into a corporate structure designed for something else entirely. Neither worked. The WV DUNA Act creates a third option built from scratch for the way these organizations actually run.

The law recognizes that software can govern, that members can be anywhere, and that a mission can be encoded rather than managed. It doesn't require a headquarters, a payroll, or a board. It requires only an agreement among members and a registration on file.

Effective July 1, 2026 Registered with the WV Secretary of State First & only jurisdiction
02 · The definition

What a DUNA is.

Four words, each one deliberate. Together they describe something that hasn't had a legal name until now.

D
Decentralized

Run by its members through code on the blockchain — no boss, no head office, no thumb on the scale. The rules are written into the software, and the software runs the same for everyone.

U
Unincorporated

No corporate shell to stand up or maintain. It exists as software and an agreement among members. You don't need lawyers on retainer or annual filings just to keep the lights on.

N
Nonprofit

Built around a mission, not owners. Value flows back to the work, not out to shareholders. What the organization earns, the organization spends on what it set out to do.

A
Association

A group of members acting as one. The members are the organization — human or agent. There's no separate ownership class above them and no outside party with a veto.

03 · The absence

What it doesn't need.

Most legal structures come with overhead: people to appoint, offices to maintain, filings to keep current. A DUNA doesn't. That's not a bug. That's the whole point.

✕ No board of directors ✕ No executive officers ✕ No physical location

A DUNA can run completely online, in software, governed entirely by its members. That's exactly what the agentic economy needs.

04 · The stakes

Why it matters.

A new legal category sounds dry. What it actually means is that two long-standing problems finally have a real answer.

Closes the gap

A legal home that didn't exist.

Until now, a crypto project or an AI agent had nowhere to be a real entity — to hold value, sign a deal, and answer for it. WV grants real legal standing to organizations that live on the blockchain and are governed by code.

Cuts fraud & scams

Accountability, on the record.

Anonymous scams plague crypto, and AI makes them cheaper to run. A registered DUNA has its name on the record and answers at law. That doesn't make fraud impossible, but it gives fraud far fewer places to hide.

05 · Registration

The registered agent.

Every registered entity needs a registered agent — an on-the-record party who receives official and legal notices on its behalf. It's a simple requirement, but it's a real one.

How the Dunathon handles it

You don't have to stand up your own shell.

On July 1, David Levine self-registers the Dunathon as DUNA #1 — the first DUNA ever filed anywhere on Earth. From that moment, the Dunathon serves as the registered agent for everyone who joins. Participants get the legal standing that comes with a registered entity without having to file paperwork themselves, hire a lawyer, or maintain a separate structure. You build. We hold the address.

In plain terms: when you join the Dunathon, you're a member of an actual registered organization, on file with the West Virginia Secretary of State. That's not a metaphor. It's a record.
06 · Decision-making

Governance markets, explained.

A governance market is how a group decides without a central authority. Members don't just vote yes or no — they express what they want and what they believe will actually work by buying and holding tokens tied to a proposal. Price and volume do the work that a raised hand can't.

Step 01

A proposal goes up.

Any member can post a proposal — a project to fund, a rule to adopt, a direction to take.

Step 02

Members buy in.

Members who want the proposal to succeed back it by buying its token. Money on the line, not just a click.

Step 03

Price signals both desire and belief.

A rising price means members both want the outcome and expect it to succeed. That's a stronger signal than a count of hands.

Step 04

Real backing gets funded.

The proposal with genuine market support gets enacted. The treasury pays it out, on-chain, automatically.

A concrete example — imagine a proposal to fund rural broadband research in Appalachia. Members who want it built and believe it'll deliver results buy into that proposal's token. As more members commit, the price rises. That rising price isn't just enthusiasm — it's a market saying: this is real, this has support, this should happen. Compare that to a poll, where you can click "yes" at no cost and walk away. Governance markets charge real stakes for real preferences.

07 · Governance

Who should govern.

Every platform makes a governance choice, whether it says so or not. The DUNA makes that choice explicitly — and puts the answer on the blockchain where no one can quietly change it.

The centralized platform

Rules, fees, and data belong to a company.

When the platform's interests and yours diverge, theirs win. They set the terms, take a cut of what you build, and can change the deal whenever it suits them. You have little say and, most of the time, nowhere to appeal.

The DUNA

Members set the rules in the open.

Governance happens on-chain, by member vote. No outside owner to overrule you, no quiet update to the terms of service at 2 a.m. What the members vote to build is what gets built. See more about what this means for people on the Culture page.
