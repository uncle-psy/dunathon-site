Economics · Tokenomics

An economy that pays for itself.

Every DUNA creates its own economy — its own token, its own treasury, its own rules. No external platform, no shareholders, no value extraction. The value stays in the network and the mission.

01 · The tokens

The tokens.

A token is the basic unit of membership — a share of governance and a stake in the mission. Holding one is how you get a say and a stake in what the DUNA does.

A DUNA token

A share of governance and a stake in the mission.

A DUNA token is not stock. It does not pay dividends, and it does not make you an owner in the for-profit sense — a DUNA is a nonprofit. What it does give you is a voice in governance and a real stake in whether the mission succeeds. Holding it is how you get a say and a stake.

Trading

Price discovers what members believe.

Tokens can be bought and sold. That means the market — the members themselves — sets the price. What people are willing to pay for a token tells you what they think a DUNA is actually worth. No outside analyst needed.

The genesis token

The Dunathon is its own DUNA.

The Dunathon is itself a DUNA, with its own token. Everyone starts here. When you register, you join DUNA #1 — the genesis — and get your first token. From there, you can join other DUNAs, co-found your own, or back the proposals you believe in.

02 · Treasuries

Each DUNA holds real funds.

A DUNA treasury is shared property — real money the members govern together. It grows through token sales and contributions. It shrinks only when members vote to pay something out. There is no CEO with a discretionary budget, and no board meeting behind closed doors.

04 · Governance

You decide what we pay for.

There is no central budget office. The treasury funds what members choose — by voting with tokens. Buying in behind a proposal is how you signal real support. The bigger the backing, the stronger the signal.

01

A member posts a proposal

Anyone at Contributor level or above can propose something — research, a project, an event, a partnership. The proposal goes on-chain for all members to see.

02

Members back it by buying in

To support a proposal, you buy tokens or stake what you hold behind it. This is the vote — not a checkbox, but real skin in the game.

03

The size of the backing signals real support

A proposal with strong token backing has demonstrated that members believe in it enough to put value behind it. Cheap votes get cheap signals; real commitment shows.

04

The treasury funds what the members chose

When a proposal clears the threshold, the treasury pays out. Every payout is recorded on the blockchain — public, permanent, and auditable by anyone.

Group votes distribute resources by buying tokens — no central budget office, no grant committee, no gatekeepers. The members are the budget.

05 · Aligned incentives

Everything does double duty.

Every choice a DUNA makes gets judged two ways at once. Good decisions pass both tests. Bad ones fail at least one — and the members can see it happen in real time.

Two questions, every time

Does it serve the mission, and does it build the value of the token?

A DUNA is a nonprofit, so the mission comes first. But the token has a price — and that price reflects what members believe the DUNA is worth. When you make decisions that serve the mission well, the token tends to follow. By design, these two things pull in the same direction. You do not have to choose between the cause and the community's stake in it.

For instance — a DUNA formed around clean water advocacy proposes to fund a river-monitoring project. The research is credible, the mission is clear, and the members believe it will build the DUNA's reputation. They back it. The treasury funds it. The token price reflects confidence in the mission — and the work gets done.
06 · Alliances

Owning each other.

DUNAs are not islands. They can invest in each other, pool resources, and back each other's proposals. The network is designed to encourage cooperation — not just between members, but between organizations.

Cross-ownership

DUNAs can buy tokens in other DUNAs.

One DUNA can hold a stake in another. That makes their missions linked — when one thrives, the other does too. It also gives DUNAs a reason to support each other's proposals, not just their own.

Alliances

Pool reach and resources.

Two or more DUNAs can form an alliance — pooling their reach, their memberships, and their treasuries behind shared proposals. Big projects that no single DUNA could fund alone become possible when several back them together.

Multiple membership

You can be in more than one DUNA at once.

There is no rule that says you belong to only one. You can be a member of several DUNAs, hold tokens in each, and vote in each. Your attention goes where you care most — and you carry as much responsibility as you choose to pick up.

07 · Founders

How founders work.

You do not need to co-found your own DUNA to have a meaningful role. You can start as a member, earn your way into the core team, or simply back the proposals you believe in and share in what they build.

Start here

Anyone joins as a member.

The default is open. Show up, register, get your token, and start participating. No invitation required, no application process, no gatekeeping. The DUNA is open by design.

Core team

Some become core team.

Over time, members who take on real responsibility — organizing the work, stewarding the treasury, carrying the accountability — earn a founder's stake. It is not granted by title. It is earned by showing up and doing the work.

No DUNA required

You do not need your own DUNA to win.

Back the proposals you believe in. Buy tokens in the DUNAs whose missions match yours. When those DUNAs succeed, you share in the outcome. The Dunathon is a collaboration — not a competition to see who launched the most organizations.

08 · The whole network

Sustaining the whole network.

Every DUNA operates on shared infrastructure — validators, tooling, and the commons that make on-chain governance possible. That infrastructure needs to be paid for. In this system, it is.

How value recirculates

No platform. No shareholders. No extraction.

The same token activity that funds each DUNA also pays to keep the shared network running — validators, tooling, and the commons. Value recirculates instead of leaking out to a middleman. Every DUNA benefits, and every DUNA contributes. That is what it means for an economy to pay for itself.
