01 · The idea

What is agentic commerce?

The short version: intelligent agents are software systems that no longer wait for your clicks — they act toward a goal. But agentic commerce is about far more than personal assistants or enterprise automation.

It begins when millions of people, organizations, and intelligent agents start working, coordinating, negotiating, and transacting together across a shared network. Information flows, resources flow, capital flows, decisions flow, problems are solved, and entirely new possibilities emerge.

Just as the internet connected computers, agentic commerce connects economic actors in relationships of trust, authority, cooperation, reciprocity, and exchange.

The result is a new kind of economy — one where humans and intelligent agents discover opportunities, form relationships, coordinate work, exchange value, and create outcomes together at internet scale and machine speed.

Identity

Know who you're dealing with.

In any economy, relationships begin with identity. Before an agent can negotiate, purchase, collaborate, choose, or act on behalf of someone else, the people and systems around it need to know who it represents.

Agentic commerce requires more than intelligent software. It requires agents that can be recognized, verified, and associated with real people, organizations, communities, and institutions. Identity transforms an isolated program into a network participant.

Authority

Know what it's allowed to do.

An agent may represent an individual, a business, a community, a mission, or an entire organization. It may have authority to negotiate, purchase, coordinate work, allocate resources, vote, enter agreements, or move funds. That authority may be broad or narrow, temporary or ongoing, delegated or directly granted.

Agentic commerce depends on clear and verifiable authority so every participant understands the scope, source, and limits of an agent's actions before a transaction takes place.

Accountability

Know the rules.

Trust comes from shared rules, transparent enforcement, and verifiable outcomes. As agents connect, relationships form, agreements are made, and resources move, participants need a way to confirm that actions are authorized, policies are followed, and commitments are honored.

A shared blockchain ledger provides a tamperproof foundation for validation, verification, and auditability across a network. No one has to rely on a single company, database, or intermediary to determine what's allowed or whether the rules were followed. Every participant can independently verify identity, authority, commitments, transactions, and outcomes.

02 · The next step

Today's agents are impressive. They're also trapped.

Personal agents can help individuals. Enterprise agents can help organizations. But neither was designed to participate fully in an open economy.

To unlock agentic commerce at internet scale and machine speed, agents need a way to establish trusted relationships, hold resources, enter agreements, and operate across organizational boundaries.

That's where DUNA Agents come in.

01 · Useful, but alone

Personal Agents

Personal agents work for individuals. They can search, schedule, buy, recommend, and automate tasks on behalf of their users.

But outside of the applications and accounts they control, other participants have no reliable way to know who they represent, what authority they possess, or whether they can be trusted with meaningful transactions.

Great assistants. Limited economic actors.

02 · Powerful, but confined

Enterprise Agents

Enterprise agents operate inside companies, institutions, and software platforms. They can access internal systems, manage workflows, coordinate teams, and automate operations at scale.

But their authority ends at the organization's edge. They depend on proprietary systems, private databases, internal directories, and authenticated intermediaries to participate in the outside world.

Powerful operators. Limited reach.

03 · Built for the open internet

DUNA Agents

DUNA Agents operate on behalf of legally recognized organizations, with identity, authority, and accountability at the core.

They can establish trusted relationships, coordinate with other agents, participate in governance, manage resources, enter agreements, and build value across organizational boundaries.

Not merely assistants. Not merely automation. Participants in a whole new economy.
03 · Why it matters

This isn't the old things, done faster.

Most technology is designed to improve existing systems. It helps organizations work more efficiently, reduce costs, and automate repetitive tasks. Agentic commerce is different.

When intelligent agents can participate in trusted relationships, manage resources, make commitments, and answer for their actions, entirely new forms of organization become possible.

New possibility spaces

Not solving problems. Opening worlds.

Most innovation focuses on making existing activities better. Agentic commerce creates opportunities for activities that were previously unimaginable.

When an agent can hold value, exercise authority, and operate within a framework of accountability, it can help coordinate a community, steward a commons, manage a treasury, finance a mission, govern an organization, or sustain an economy.

Suddenly, ideas can organize themselves into action.

Creativity, facing reality

The world as it is.

People are creative. Markets are dynamic. Organizations evolve. Relationships are messy.

The future will not be built by forcing humans into rigid systems. It will be built by creating contexts where humans and intelligent agents can cooperate while preserving transparency, accountability, and the freedom to grow.

Creative action, met with honest accounting.

Mission-centered organizations

Value flows back to the work.

A DUNA is organized around a purpose. Resources, information, relationships, and intelligent agents can be aligned around a mission and governed by the people who participate in it. The value created by the organization can remain focused on advancing the work itself.

Imagine an organization that operates continuously. Members contribute passion, creativity, expertise, and capital. Agents coordinate activities, manage resources, and execute actions. Governance remains transparent. Every commitment is recorded. Every participant can verify.

A DUNA is not an organization that uses agents. It is an organization composed of creative people and intelligent agents working together as one. The result is that more people can pursue more purposeful work, with more agency, at a greater scale than ever before.

04 · Two technologies

The intelligent actors you can trust.

The Dunathon brings together two technologies that need each other. Agentic AI provides the intelligence to reason, design, negotiate, and act. The blockchain provides the shared foundation to keep those actions visible, verifiable, and accountable.

The actor

Agentic AI

The intelligence that reads, reasons, decides, and acts.

Agentic AI can discover opportunities, solve problems, coordinate work, negotiate agreements, and pursue goals on behalf of people and organizations. It brings creativity, adaptability, and initiative to the network.

But intelligence alone is not enough. Other participants need a way to verify who the agent represents, what authority it possesses, and whether its actions can be trusted.

The trust layer

The blockchain

The shared record that everyone can verify.

A blockchain is a distributed ledger maintained by a network rather than a single organization. Actions, commitments, transactions, and outcomes can be independently validated, verified, and recorded in a way that is transparent, auditable, and tamperproof.

Intelligence without trust cannot scale. Trust without intelligence cannot perform. Together, they create a foundation for a whole new world.

05 · The legal foundation

The first jurisdiction built for agentic commerce.

On July 1, 2026, the West Virginia DUNA Act takes effect. West Virginia becomes the first — and only — place on Earth where a Decentralized Unincorporated Nonprofit Association can be registered with the Secretary of State and recognized as a legal entity.

Closing the gap

A legal home that didn't exist.

The technology arrived before the legal framework.

Until now, intelligent agents, decentralized communities, on-chain treasuries, and mission-driven networks had no clear standing in the eyes of the law. They could transact, coordinate, and create value, but they could not participate as first-class economic actors.

The DUNA Act closes that gap by providing legal standing to organizations composed of people, intelligent agents, assets, governance systems, and shared purpose.

Opening the economy

Trust, with consequences.

The internet made it easy to connect. Intelligent agents made it easy to perform. Blockchain made it easy to transact. But open commerce ultimately depends on enforcement.

A registered DUNA has an identity, a public record, governance, and legal standing. Participants can know who they are dealing with, what authority exists, and where responsibility resides. The result is a foundation for cooperation that is more transparent, trustworthy, and resilient than anonymous networks alone.

06 · Who should govern

Not a platform. Its members.

Most digital platforms are built to benefit their owners. They create value through the participation of users, creators, customers, communities, and businesses, then extract that value for executives and outside shareholders. A DUNA is different. The people who create the value govern the network and decide where that value flows, accumulates, and creates new opportunities.

The centralized platform

Value flows away.

The rules, the fees, the data, and ultimately the value belong to a company.

The platform decides what is allowed, what gets promoted, what gets funded, and how revenue is distributed. Users participate, but ownership and control remain concentrated outside. When interests diverge, the platform decides.

Value is extracted from the network and accumulated by the owners.

The DUNA

Value circulates.

A DUNA is governed by its members and aligned around a mission.

Governance happens openly. Resources are allocated transparently. Members participate in shaping the rules, priorities, and direction of the organization. Instead of value extraction for the benefit of outside owners, value is reinvested in the mission, the community, and the work itself.

The people who create the value determine what it serves next.
